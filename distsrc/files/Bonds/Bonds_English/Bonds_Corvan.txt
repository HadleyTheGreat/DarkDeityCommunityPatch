Aurima and Corvan
Stage 1


Corvan
Aurima, pardon me, erm... I've read so much of your exploits-- and now to finally meet you, well, suffice it to say I'm honored you've decided to accompany us.
Aurima
You're shaking like a wet dog, elf. Have some dignity. 
Corvan
Why-- oh. I suppose I am. Perhaps there's a draft.
Aurima
Mm. So you are the one who led the others to my trial.
Corvan
Yes. And it was no trivial task. You did not make yourself easily discoverable.
Aurima
If I had, I expect you might have found me too late.
Corvan
Before another challenger bested you? I hardly think it likely.
Aurima
I do not claim to have the power to single handedly stem the tide of the armies of Aramor.
Corvan
Would armies have been sent after you?
Aurima
There are those with the power to send them who stand to gain much satisfaction from my death, even still.
Corvan
You speak of those responsible for your exile.
Aurima
I speak of whom I speak.
Corvan
Perhaps that was presumptuous of me to ask. Might we start at the beginning, then? 
Corvan
What was it like to grow up under the shadow of your father, the great Mirage of the Silent Sands?
Aurima
And how should I share my story with you, when I have yet to finish writing it myself?
Corvan
We all must share our stories while we yet live, or we risk them being rewritten by other, less reliable narrators.
Aurima
Is that a threat?
Corvan
Of course not. Merely a humble historian's advice.
Aurima
Hm. Come, then. Let us go where we might speak more privately. I will tell you of my father.
Corvan
Wonderful!


Stage 2

Corvan
Ah, I do enjoy these moments of respite. Without them I'd hardly have a chance to pick up a pen.
Aurima
And with them I hardly have a chance to catch my breath.
Corvan
Shall we resume where we left off last time?
Aurima
If you start by reminding me where that was.
Corvan
I believe you were about to recount your match with Yerassi, the Proud.
Aurima
Hah. Yes. A fitting epithet. He was the first highborn challenger I ever contended with, though he would not be the last.
Aurima
I still remember his entrance with such clarity. He could hardly tear his gaze from the crowds to face me.
Aurima
It would have been a short match if that was my wish, but I pitied him. I wanted to give him a show for his noble friends. I thought maybe then he could return with his dignity.
Aurima
But I was an ignorant fool. Every minute the match dragged on and I evaded his blows, the crowds began to think I was taunting him, and his face grew twisted with humiliation.
Aurima
It became clear the only path to restore his pride would be to throw the match.
Corvan
I don't imagine you seriously considered that for long.
Aurima
No. Never seriously. Once I realized I had backed myself into a corner, I decided to put an end to it swiftly.
Corvan
Fascinating. You won't be surprised to know the rumormongers tell it differently.
Aurima
No, I'm not. 
Aurima
I'm sure you heard it was a vicious scrap-- the closest Aurima the Cenaari ever came to death-- that I crippled his leg and disgraced my honor to rescue victory from the jaws of certain defeat.
Corvan
Not so poetically, but yes, I heard something like that.
Aurima
Well, decide the truth for yourself. I will tell my part.
Aurima
I did strike him down with a blow to the leg, but he walked from that arena on his own two feet. I thought it strange when he did not join us at the baths that afternoon.
Aurima
A few days later, I heard the same rumors which I'm sure made their way to you: 
Aurima
That I had shattered his foot with a desperate blow, that the healers had no choice but to sever his leg at the knee, that he would not walk again, let alone fight, as long as he lived.
Corvan
If the rumors were false, would the people of Oasis not have known so as soon as Yerassi made his next public appearance?
Aurima
The rumors were partially true. His leg was severed.
Corvan
... Then you suspect he inflicted it on himself?
Aurima
Man will go to great lengths for his pride. And I have seen greater lengths than that.
Corvan
By Quintare... I don't know what to say. It's just unbelievable enough to be true.
Aurima
A strange way of looking at it, but I suppose so.
Corvan
The only way of looking at it. Yes... this is a story, indeed.
Aurima
Do me one favor please, Corvan. If you do decide to publish this, be sure I'm nowhere near Oasis.
Corvan
Mm. Well. Your exile should help with that.
Aurima
Hmph.


Stage 3

Aurima
Corvan, I've grown accustomed to your visits. I'm expecting you to come clamoring for the truth about my exile.
Corvan
Hm? Oh. Yes. That itch has not left me. But I'll confess, this research into Akmenos and his staff has me a bit overwhelmed.
Aurima
Anything I can assist with?
Corvan
Not beyond what you've offered already. You did much to set us on the right path with the Aspects.
Aurima
I'm glad to have helped. I only wish there was more I could do.
Corvan
Well, you don't happen to have an extensive knowledge of soul anchors and their history, do you?
Aurima
That is a subject in which I would easily trust your expertise over mine.
Corvan
Mm. It was worth a shot.
Aurima
Always is.
Corvan
I do wish our recent days afforded time for old tales. 
Aurima
We'll simply have to forge ahead until we reach ones that do.
Corvan
Indeed. And then I will learn what really drove the longest standing champion of the arena from the height of his glory.
Aurima
The height of that time.
Corvan
Yes. Our greatest accomplishments lie beyond us, certainly.
Aurima
I hope so. But I'm glad to know I'll have my personal biographer assisting me in both making and chronicling those accomplishments.
Corvan
I would not have it otherwise. Our stories will be crucial to the world we hope to rebuild.
Aurima
Then we should hold nothing back in our examples.
Corvan
I very much intend not to.


Corvan and Vesta

Vesta
It seems you hardly ever part with that quill, Corvan.
Corvan
Inspiration is a fickle lover. A faithful writer must be ready to reach for his pen at a moment's notice.
Vesta
And what about an unfaithful writer?
Corvan
I wouldn't know. I've never read any of their work.
Vesta
Fair point. 
Corvan
On the subject of inspiration, your background has sparked my curiosity. There are a few questions I might ask, if you had the time.
Vesta
I figured as much.
Corvan
Tell me, the trove of Aspects you kept at the temple, did you guard them alone? Or were there others with you?
Vesta
Corvan, I'm no hermit. Of course there were others. Those who supported my mission, the clerics who sent me to this temple, and so on.
Corvan
Naturally, but I mean actually with you, in the temple.
Vesta
Some local folk. All rather lovely. Merchants, farmers, travelers - pilgrims from all over Etlan. I grew fond of them.
Corvan
And yet, all that while, your mission was hidden from them.
Vesta
The defense of the Aspects was not my only task, Corvan. I am a cleric. My duty was to tend to the faithful.
Corvan
Of course. No doubt they relied on you to inspire their faith. And who inspired yours?
Vesta
One who prays is never far from the divine. 
Corvan
... 
Vesta
I'll admit, it was difficult at times, but there were little moments now and again that reminded me of my purpose, why I did what I did. Those moments sustained me.
Vesta
What interests you so much about my past, anyway?
Corvan
Just something I've always wondered about clerics. So many lean on them. How can they stand on their own two feet?
Vesta
Same as we march onwards now - together, supporting one another. It's no mystery, really.
Corvan
Good. In that case, our success depends on a proven strategy.
Vesta
I believe it does.
Corvan
Then may we all take inspiration from your faith.


Stage 2

Corvan
Impressive craftsmanship, Vesta. I didn't take you for a seamstress.
Vesta
You'd be right not to. This is amateur work. I've hardly improved since my mother first taught me.
Corvan
And what's this here?
Vesta
A trim for Samara's hood. In my time in Aramor, I tried to learn the patterns of their craft. Their designs are quite intricate.
Corvan
Yes. I've studied their geometric architecture extensively.
Vesta
Of course you have.
Corvan
Nothing surprises you anymore, does it?
Vesta
On the contrary, Corvan. You're full of surprises. I suppose that's what I've gotten used to.
Corvan
A woman of self-contained paradoxes.
Vesta
That sounds a lot better than cracked in the head. 
Vesta
Speaking of surprises, I suspect some of my prayers of late have been misplaced.
Corvan
Oh? Our priestess having a crisis of faith?
Vesta
Your priestess is discovering the almighty hand of Soleric hasn't been striking down foes all around her, or at least not as consistently as the quick-bladed Corvan Gallivar.
Corvan
Ah, I see. Perhaps you're not wrong to thank Soleric. Have you considered I might be the answer to your prayers?
Vesta
Wouldn't that just go right to your head...
Corvan
It would be my greatest honor to serve the gods.
Vesta
For your sake, I hope the gods don't understand sarcasm.
Corvan
Oh I'm sure they do. I think I'm better off hoping they also have a sense of humor.
Vesta
Mm. Well if my life's any evidence, they certainly have that.
Corvan
Yes. I believe they do.


Stage 3

Vesta
By Vosh, Corvan, you look terrible.
Corvan
Here I came to the priestess for words of kindness. Now she's made me the fool.
Vesta
You've made a fool of yourself concealing these wounds. What's happened to your arm?
Corvan
Oh, just a knick. Nothing that won't heal right up.
Vesta
So long as someone heals it.
Corvan
Vesta, please. 
Vesta
Corvan...
Corvan
Very well. You are stubborn, you know.
Vesta
I'm the stubborn one?
Vesta
... 
Vesta
There. Better, isn't it?
Corvan
If you must know.
Vesta
I can patch that bracer up too, if you like.
Corvan
Now really, that's not necessary.
Vesta
No. Not at all. You've learned to fight with both hands, anyway. You won't miss one.
Corvan
I can see I'm not getting out of this either. 
Vesta
No. Let me have a look.
Vesta
... 
Vesta
That's worse than I thought. Give me some time with it and I'll have something workable before you need it again.
Corvan
I sometimes wonder where we'd be without our priestess.
Vesta
Don't. You'll give yourself nightmares.
Corvan
Hah. You're all too right about that. What we're dealing with by day is nightmarish enough.
Vesta
Then be glad nightmares have ends. The world will soon awaken to a bright dawn.
Corvan
If we can help it.
Vesta
And we can. But only if we allow ourselves to rely on one another.
Corvan
We're not hermits, after all.
Vesta
No, we're not.
Corvan
Well, you know where to find me.
Vesta
When you're not holed up in a tree with your notebook.
Corvan
I feel so known!
Vesta
I'm sure you do.


Corvan and Maeve

Corvan
Picture my surprise when the only half-elven inquisitor showed up to the council meeting. I've wanted to meet you for a long time.
Maeve
Oh? And what, pray tell, do you know of me that would merit such a desire?
Corvan
Aside from my cultural sensitivities and interest in the historically significant?
Maeve
Yes, aside from those things.
Corvan
Aside from the reasonable desire to know one's travelling companions and become friends?
Maeve
Point taken, Corvan, aside from those things. You have a look of recognition in your eyes that goes beyond any text.
Corvan
Mmm, you truly do the stories justice. Do you happen to know my surname?
Maeve
I take it you'll tell me if I don't?
Corvan
Quite right! My surname is that of a long running Moon Elf clan - Gallivar!
Maeve
YOU'RE Cormac's little nephew, Corvan?
Corvan
That I am, and we grew close before my travels to Etlan.
Corvan
He told me much of your time together.
Maeve
What a small world, nothing too bad, I hope?
Corvan
Nothing bad? The old elf practically worships you!
Maeve
No! He saved my hide just as much as I saved him! I spent a hundred years getting the best education on the continent, and he still knew a far sight more than I did.
Corvan
Well, he has, what, nearly a century of life on you?
Maeve
I can't quite remember. Hard to keep track - he presented himself much younger than he actually was.
Corvan
Yes, that he does, doesn't he? I'd love to pick your brain when we have a little more time.
Maeve
I think I'd be delighted for you to do that. It's been too long since I spent any real time with your kin.
Corvan
Excellent!


Stage 2

Maeve
Corvan! Tell me, how did you come to find yourself among this ragtag crew of humans?
Corvan
Funnily enough, it all comes back to my research.
Maeve
I had no doubt of that, I'm more interested in the details.
Corvan
Of course, of course. Well, Sara and Samara had taken an interest in the Aspects, and I was staying at the royal palace in Aramor for a time.
Corvan
I was combing through the great library looking for information on the calamity temples on Etlan - a much different variety from the ones that are found on Verroa.
Maeve
Not that different. It's generally agreed upon that the same civilization built all of them, regardless of location.
Corvan
Ah, yes, but they used local materials - local styles. There is a great deal of variety even from nation to nation - lines on a map that no doubt didn't exist at the time.
Maeve
I see, I read of this but never really considered it of note.
Corvan
Most feel that way, but I had my suspicions that it might come into play. As the Order has so kindly neglected to tell us - there seem to be more Aspects on Etlan than in Verroa.
Maeve
Yes, I noticed that. What do you think is causing them to hold that information back?
Corvan
Oh, you've no idea how nice it is to be among my own kind for once - intellectuals, I mean.
Corvan
There must have been some seismic change, long long ago, where one of the Order grandmasters made the call to move them.
Corvan
From what I could gather in Oasis, the Etlan temples were never originally the holding place for them. You know what that tells me?
Maeve
I don't know that I do.
Corvan
Somebody was digging. Someone had them shook up enough to move them across the whole world - a prospect infinitely more dangerous than leaving them in hiding.
Maeve
There haven't been any major situations with the Aspects in a long time.
Corvan
Maybe they simply made the smart move moving them. Plenty of upheavals are caused by a single Aspect - these times where so many are disappearing are unprecedented.
Maeve
I see it! Whoever is taking them now must have had access to a great deal of historical information to know where to go after them.
Corvan
Indeed, and perhaps more privileged archives as well.
Maeve
Perhaps... You've given me a good deal to think on, and I prefer to do so alone. We'll talk soon. 


Stage 3

Maeve
This staff is running me in circles, Corvan. I need help with something.
Corvan
Of course, anything you need. What is the trouble in particular?
Maeve
We've identified the composition, at least partially, but how in the world has it held up for so long? It seems as if it were just ripped off the bark?
Corvan
Perhaps the soul magic that infuses it allows it to retain vigor? I am unsure.
Maeve
What I wouldn't give to have Vesta in here right now.
Corvan
I can go grab her if you think it would help?
Maeve
No... She's with the wounded - that's more important than a hunch with something that might not matter regardless.
Maeve
I've never come across a problem I couldn't solve - nothing even close to this.
Corvan
Oh, but that can't be true. You're classically educated amongst the Threllisar elite schools - don't they teach impossible thought as early as forty?
Maeve
Well yes, but those are intended to be unsolvable.
Corvan
Ah, then you didn't understand the tradition of the exercise.
Maeve
How do you mean?
Corvan
Your schools teach impossible thought as a tool - a way to refine your process and understand when you aren't capable of tackling a problem.
Corvan
Useful, yes, but not the pure intent of the exercise.
Corvan
The problems presented in traditional versions of impossible thought actually are solvable - we simply don't know how yet. It is not intended to teach you when to stop, but rather how to continue.
Maeve
I can't help but feel cheated...
Corvan
No point, what you learned was equally valuable - simply not for our current situation.
Maeve
Right, we should focus up.
Corvan
What a delightful treat to be able to speak on that to someone who understands. I've missed my time in Verroa dearly.
Maeve
But you wouldn't trade the adventure of it all?
Corvan
No, no I wouldn't... Let's get to it - we have a lot to learn.

Corvan and Thae'Lanel
Stage 1

Thae'Lanel
I am pleased to find one of your kind among our current travelling companions.
Corvan
The same goes to you, Thae'Lanel. The Exiled are a rare sight indeed - nowadays especially.
Thae'Lanel
We have built an incredible life for ourselves such that we need not lend our services as often.
Corvan
So I've read - tell me, how many of your people are contracted out at the moment?
Thae'Lanel
Even if I know, I would not tell you. I'm sure you understand that matters of Fauldaren's security are taken as carefully as possible.
Corvan
Of course, of course. My interest is purely academic, I assure you.
Thae'Lanel
Many of my people have found themselves dissected by interests 'purely academic'. I believe you, truly, but I am cautious nonetheless. That is my privilege and my curse.
Corvan
Fascinating the loyalty you feel to Fauldaren. You carry a great deal of responsibility with you as a representative to the world.
Thae'Lanel
Yes, I do. I have done so for a long, long time. I intend to continue doing so until I no longer can.
Corvan
How much of your income does Fauldaren keep?
Thae'Lanel
That... Is a question I can answer, for my answer is different from the other exiled.
Thae'Lanel
I keep none of my income, save for that which I require to sustain myself. When my trips are completed and I return home, I unload all of my earnings that I still retain.
Corvan
Incredible... How do you sustain yourself when you're home, then?
Thae'Lanel
I am... Of a certain... I am unsure how to explain. Things are different in Fauldaren - we do not have use for money quite as the other cultures of this world do.
Corvan
You share things freely?
Thae'Lanel
Not quite - we do have currency. I have earned such a rank among my people so that I am allowed what I need. If I head to the market and request something, it is simply given.
Corvan
Then you are royalty among your people?
Thae'Lanel
A funny thought, but no. I have spent centuries bringing prosperity to Fauldaren - at great risk to my personal safety.
Corvan
And so they respect that, and you are taken care of... The cultural unity is pleasing to hear of, I must admit.
Thae'Lanel
Mmm. It truly is, though as you know, it does not come free.
Corvan
Of course not...


Stage 2

Thae'Lanel
Might I ask you of your home?
Corvan
Of course. I miss the Verroan mountains dearly.
Thae'Lanel
My apologies, I mean more of your childhood than the locale itself.
Corvan
Ah, but the two are linked in my mind. Asking of one brings visions of the other.
Thae'Lanel
Understandable, I suppose either is interesting enough, if not equal in my intent to reveal.
Thae'Lanel
I was curious about your customs regarding the visual arts. Many know of my people's appreciation for such things, but I've never heard the same said of the Moon Elves.
Corvan
An interesting question, if I do say so myself. I don't believe I've ever fielded a personal question about artistry.
Corvan
Moon Elves appreciate art, but my people have a singular focus. History is unyielding, and covers all subjects - this means that there are those among us whose priorities are cataloguing important art.
Corvan
My uncle, Cormac, was such an elf. His studies leaned towards the arts.
Corvan
But to my knowledge, not that he ever made much sense to me, he studied art in a purely historic fashion - he knew how to look for beauty and quality.
Thae'Lanel
But cared little for them save for their ability to help him catalogue?
Corvan
Precisely.
Thae'Lanel
I think that our peoples could learn a great deal from one another.
Corvan
I think we all know that and simply don't want to admit it.
Thae'Lanel
I'm not so sure that this realization is consistent. I have heard unkind words spoken of the Moon Elves too many times.
Corvan
Understanding and realization I have found come in different waves. They will understand one day.
Thae'Lanel
I hope so. I have a great deal of respect for you and your kind.
Corvan
And I for yours. The fear that the Exiled are treated with has always struck me as strange. Who are we to judge other people's culture, when our own has a myriad of concerns as well?
Thae'Lanel
It has taken me many years to come to terms with the revulsion I bring everywhere that I go. People are afraid of that which they do not understand.
Thae'Lanel
That is simple enough on its own, but they also fear that which is different from themselves. These two together brew a hatred that burns restlessly.
Corvan
Too true. You have a kind soul, Thae'Lanel. I hope you know that.
Thae'Lanel
Aleithos says the same...


Stage 3

Corvan
You mentioned before that you have entered a soul anchor...
Thae'Lanel
Yes. This was not my first encounter with such a thing.
Corvan
You mentioned that it is forbidden to speak of. I would like to hear and record the story. I promise you that I will not allow its release until after you have passed.
Thae'Lanel
...
Corvan
I understand that I am asking something that goes against your code.
Thae'Lanel
No. I will do this. I have lived a thousand legendary moments, none of which have been recorded.
Thae'Lanel
This story deserves to be heard. Knowing that someone may draw courage from it in the future - that is enough to bypass my code.
Corvan
Please - go ahead.
Thae'Lanel
Nearly three centuries ago, I was contracted as a bodyguard for a powerful councilman in Naja - someone who had aggravated the wrong mystics.
Thae'Lanel
This man, who shall remain nameless, became my friend. We spent every waking moment together, as the threats to his safety were constant. He was the first outside of my home that I grew to respect.
Thae'Lanel
He taught me of the world, opened my eyes to the prejudice I saw other cultures with. He changed the course of my life, and in doing so saved the lives of many others.
Thae'Lanel
When the attack came, it was furious. I was yet to achieve the peak of my abilities, and could not hold them off. He was taken.
Thae'Lanel
The mystic who was after him had prolonged his life - much like I believe Akmenos had done - consuming souls of the wealthy.
Thae'Lanel
She had painted wealth as the peak of evil, and used that sentiment to feed an evil of her own. I would not allow my friend to suffer the same fate as his predecessors.
Thae'Lanel
I share a deep bond with my wife, Aleithos - one bound by the ancient magic of our blood. We can communicate over long distances, and she strengthens me in times of need.
Thae'Lanel
I gave chase to the mystic, who fled into the anchor. Still, I chased. I was consumed by the magic of the stone.
Thae'Lanel
Aleithos was my link to the mortal plane - with me as if there in body and soul. She linked our minds, and I believe our souls as well, in that moment.
Thae'Lanel
It gave me enough time to march to my friend, who I dragged out - ragged and out of his mind. We escaped the anchor, and he lived a long life. He never regained control of his mind.
Thae'Lanel
The power of my bond with Aleithos was all that allowed me to retain my sanity and mortality when I entered the anchor. I'm not sure that any other could have survived the power of the artifact.
Thae'Lanel
It was my proudest moment, when I found the will to sacrifice my own life to save a man who I owed nothing. I have tried to live up to it since.
Corvan
A beautiful story.
Thae'Lanel
One with a final note. When I returned to Fauldaren, I found Aleithos had slipped into a deep coma - awakening several weeks after my return.
Thae'Lanel
Even with a magic-strengthened bond and the most powerful blood on Terrazael, that anchor nearly killed both myself and Aleithos.
Corvan
Incredible... Are you alright?
Thae'Lanel
I am... saddened. Our bond is weakening as we age, and I travel further. We cannot commune as we once did. The lack of her presence now is like missing an arm.
Corvan
I'm so sorry...
Thae'Lanel
Don't be. We have lived four hundred beautiful years together. All things must end in time.
Corvan
You have lived a beautiful life.
Thae'Lanel
I have. I hope the same for you. I would call you friend.


Corvan and Fenton

That was me making a request.

Write? If my people wrote all their histories down, there'd not be a stone left on Etlan.
Corvan
Yes, well, perhaps that's why paper is my medium of choice.

Leaflets are only good for blowing in the wind. If it's worth writing down, it should be written in stone.
Corvan
But as you've correctly pointed out, that would present a problem for long works like histories.

That's why we sing ours. A book can be destroyed, or cast aside. But walk on the roads in Everhearth, and you'll hear the songs of our ancestors, whether you intend to or not.

I know a fair number of them myself. If you insist, I might be able to muster my strength, but you'd best make yourself comfortable. They're each of them quite long.
Corvan
Oh that's quite alright. I think I'll--
Fenton
Ahem-- Before the mountains rose up from the earth, when gemstones shone with none to judge their worth...


Stage 2

Fenton
Ah, Corvan, there you are! You've such a natural gift for stealth, I'd have judged you were hiding from me if I didn't know you better.
Corvan
Hiding? How absurd... 
Fenton
Now I've had a mind to ask what you thought of that history I sang for you. Hoh, you should've seen yourself, must've drifted off a wee bit just at the end there.

You looked just like a dwarfling the morning after his nameday - conked out cold. I'd have asked what you thought then, but you looked as peaceful as a babe. I'd have hated to disturb you.
Corvan
Ah, yes, forgive me. If I recall, that was a long day.
Fenton
Not a problem. In fact, I'd say I'm warmed up. I can sing that last bit again if you fancy.
Corvan
Oh, that won't be necessary. Come to think of it, I think I remember it quite well. Yes, it was striking.
Fenton
Aye, wasn't it? Greatest  bit of the song, some say, and I might agree. Leads right up to the lords who rule over the great dwarven houses today.
Corvan
That is quite an extensive history. It's a wonder you remember it all.
Fenton
They're hard to escape back home.
Corvan
Do you suspect the songs have changed as they were handed down? Of course, new history is added, but what about the earlier lines? Have any bits of history become legend?
Fenton
Not in the slightest. The songs weren't meant to be sung alone. Each one has at least a dozen harmonies. When one dwarf strays from the lyrics, it's impossible not to notice.
Corvan
Fascinating.
Fenton
Don't you have any songs in the Expanse?
Corvan
We do, but not so much in the place of recorded histories. Though we do have some historical art. Poems mostly.
Fenton
I don't know that I've heard a poem.
Corvan
It's a kind of... hm. Somewhat difficult to describe actually. It's a type of artistic writing, where the words can rhyme, though they don't have to, and they're often spoken.
Fenton
Sounds like a song to me.
Corvan
